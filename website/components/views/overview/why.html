Why predict churn, and why this question

A music subscription earns money only while people keep renewing. If a service knows before a plan runs out who is likely to leave, it can spend its retention budget on them instead of on everyone.

Why early

A warning helps only while there is time to act, so every feature uses data up to a fixed cutoff. Our main cutoff, 31 March, comes after most plans have expired, so its score is an upper bound. We also cut billing at 1 March, before any labelled plan expires.

Why listening, not just billing

A business already watches billing. Listening shows engagement: someone who stops playing music may leave even with auto-renew on. Does that add a signal billing lacks?

Why it is hard

Only 9% of subscribers churn, so a model can look accurate while catching nobody. Many profiles are incomplete (60% of ages missing). And the billing window overlaps the label window, which makes the task look easier than it is.
