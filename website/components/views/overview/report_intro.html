From the question to the conclusion.

Using only what is known on 31 March 2017, can we predict which KKBOX subscribers whose plan expires in March 2017 will leave — and does what they listen to say more than what they pay?

Target is_churn: no new valid subscription within 30 days of that plan's expiry. Unit: one subscriber, 946,686 of them; 9% churn. The test split was scored once.

Inputs: profile, billing up to 31 March and March listening; nothing dated later. The 31 March score is an upper bound, so we also cut billing at 1 March. One cohort, association not cause. What the model may use

The answer in short

  1. 01 Very predictable on 31 March, but that is an upper bound.

    PR-AUC 0.9644 [0.9624, 0.9664], against 0.3164 for a one-line rule. But March billing often already records the renewal; with billing cut at 1 March it reaches 0.5682.

    Results
  2. 02 Billing does almost all the work.

    Billing alone reaches 0.9562. Listening adds only +0.0083 [0.0073, 0.0093].

    Ablation
  3. 03 Listening matters more when billing is hidden, partly.

    Cut billing at 1 March and listening is worth +0.1474 [0.1426, 0.1524], eighteen times more, though part of it may come from listening after a plan has lapsed.

    Strict check
